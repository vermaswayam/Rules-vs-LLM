### How the expected answers were built

The initial 756-case core was written before the production engine. The frozen corpus was then expanded to cover deduction-cap boundaries and the ends of rebate marginal-relief intervals. `project_a/tests/oracle.py` uses independent closed-form cumulative slab bases; production integrates bands and emits a ledger. `scripts/build_tax_fixtures.py` is fixture-authoring code, deliberately excluded from reproduction. The corpus includes +/-1, +/-5 and +/-11 around slab, rebate and surcharge boundaries, age 59/60/79/80, residents/nonresidents, HRA periods, health and loan caps. Manual anchors independently check INR 71,500 for FY24 gross salary 12 lakh and zero for FY25 gross salary 12.75 lakh. Independent official-asset checks cover net-income arithmetic on 30 profiles; shared interpretation errors in unverified deduction eligibility remain possible.

Final tax uses section 288B nearest-INR-10 rounding; the pre-rounding rupee/decimal amount is retained. Section 288A income rounding happens before slab tax. A claim of rupee exactness means equality of the integer payable, not floating-point tolerance.

### Plain-language handling

The deterministic parser accepts a deliberately narrow high-precision vocabulary including lakh/crore, monthly amounts, Hinglish and AY-to-FY conversion. It requires year, gross-salary period, age, tax residency and an explicit statement about deductions. It returns questions for HRA, medical or housing eligibility, incomplete inputs, CTC/take-home and conflicts. Complex inputs are completed through structured fields. It never passes an ambiguous description into the tax engine. The 50 examples are a small, hand-labelled development corpus, not a general language benchmark.

### Official validation

`project_a/tests/fixtures/official_checks.json` contains 30 checked salary profiles and both official outputs. The deployed official site asset `data/official_calculator/calculator.js` was executed unchanged in an isolated Node VM, with UI state supplied by a thin wrapper. Raw official outputs, input binding and asset hash are saved; `scripts/verify_official.py` replays them on every run. All 60 answers agree after converting the official rupee-rounded display to statutory section 288B final rounding. This is independent official-calculator-code validation, not manual browser interaction. The calculator accepts net taxable income, so it does not independently verify HRA or deduction eligibility. Re-execute with `node scripts/run_official.mjs` when Node is installed.

## Final design for 100,000 users (under two pages)

**Ship a rule-based calculation core.** It passed the full frozen corpus with identical repeated answers, and its signed tax ledger reconciles to payable. Gemini returned both tax amounts exactly on 10.00% of 200 measured profiles in the fixed 200-case cohort. Its median API latency was 10,126.697 ms and its explanation is not a reconciling ledger. The model cohort is a targeted boundary sample, not a population estimate.

**Input: rule validation, optionally hybrid extraction later.** Keep the structured form and conservative parser. Ask the user to confirm extracted facts and deduction eligibility before calculating. An LLM could widen language coverage, but that is not implemented or measured as a winning component here. It must supply evidence spans and nulls for unknowns; it must not invent residency, a salary period or eligible expenses. Employer NPS, professional tax, arrears, foreign tax and other heads are outside this deliberately stated scope.

**Policy preparation: hybrid authoring with deterministic execution.** The compiler emits a small JSON policy, not arbitrary code. Schema checks and the frozen suite reject bad candidates; the runtime never promotes them automatically. The bundled Codex candidate demonstrates the pattern. Because both policies share the interpreter, identical results establish that candidate, not general trust in an LLM compiler. Eligibility logic such as HRA and 80D remains readable code and must be reviewed when law changes.

**Budget updates: versioned rule deployment.** Pin source hashes, financial year and policy hash to every calculation. Add next-year fixtures and official calculator observations before deployment, diff every changed field, and replay old-year cases to ensure they stay unchanged. Include deliberate stale-slab/rebate mutations and require the gate to fail. Run the old and proposed policies in shadow mode, review unexpected deltas, and retain a rollback artifact. A new year is never inferred from today's date.

**Operations:** Cache immutable policies once per worker. Keep request arithmetic stateless, store only consented inputs and the output ledger, and avoid model calls on the request path. Total registered users alone does not determine load; capacity planning needs peak calculations/second. Benchmark the HTTP service under that workload before making an SLA claim. Source-linked ledgers make support and interview explanations possible without relying on a generated paragraph.
